# Demo story — Maya, age 15

**Demo data only.** Maya is fictional. No real patient information or live
medical records are used.

## The story

Maya is 15 and sometimes forgets her medicine on weekends and sleepovers.
She wants support that feels private—not like her parent is monitoring her.

Maya and her caregiver answer the barrier check-in separately. Their
responses differ. The prototype treats that difference as a cue for the
care team to follow up; it does not show Maya’s private answer to her
caregiver.

Maya chooses a reminder at a time that works for her and a routine she
already has. The plan has a named owner: the transplant coordinator.
The coordinator sees the plan and a check-in due within two weeks.

At follow-up, the team records whether the plan is **Working**, **Not
working**, or needs to be **Adjusted**. If the plan is not working, that
signals a conversation—not a reason to blame Maya.

## What to show

1. Maya’s barrier check-in.
2. A separate caregiver response.
3. The coordinator’s view of the response difference, without exposing
   Maya’s private answer to the caregiver.
4. Maya’s agreed reminder plan and named owner.
5. The due date and follow-up status.

If the prototype includes chart-risk flags, point out that any shown
patient data is fictional demo data, not a live chart connection.

## Important boundary

The prototype helps organize the barrier → plan → follow-up loop. It
does not change medication instructions or replace a trained clinician’s
conversation.
